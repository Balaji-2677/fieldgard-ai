⚙️ The End-to-End Machine Learning Architecture

1. Agricultural Data Ingestion

Calibrated with historical crop reports from the Directorate of Economics and Statistics (DES) and ICAR. Covers 12 major crops, 12 states, 4 seasons, and 6 soil types.

2. Feature Pipeline Preprocessor

Categorical features (Crop, State, Season, Soil Type) pass through OneHotEncoder. Numerical features (Area, Rainfall, Temperature, Fertilizer, Pesticide) are scaled with StandardScaler.

3. Random Forest Regressor

An ensemble of 160 decision trees trained with bootstrap aggregating. Effectively captures complex, non-linear interactions between weather stress, soil fertility, and crop physiology.

4. Agronomic Advisory Engine

Predictions are compared against regional yield benchmarks. Automated heuristics evaluate soil compatibility and climate risks, issuing actionable tips for water and nutrients.

Frequently Asked Questions for Farmers

Practical questions about using AI for farm planning

❓ How does the model distinguish between different crops?

Different crops have vastly different biological limits. For instance, Wheat yields typically range between 2.5 to 5.5 tonnes/ha, whereas Sugarcane yields range between 55 to 95 tonnes/ha. Our Random Forest pipeline incorporates one-hot encoded crop features to preserve individual crop profiles.

❓ Can I convert the yield to Acres and Quintals?

Yes! On the prediction page, you can toggle your farm area between Hectares and Acres. The system automatically converts the predicted yield into both standard Tonnes / Hectare and traditional Quintals / Acre, as well as total farm production.

❓ Can I print the prediction result to show my local agricultural officer or bank?

Yes! After generating a prediction, click the 🖨️ Print Farmer Yield Card button. The website formats a clean, official single-page summary suitable for printing or saving as a PDF report.

❓ Where does the rainfall and temperature data come from?

Farmers can input their seasonal average temperature and cumulative rainfall based on local IMD (India Meteorological Department) weather forecasts or historical district rainfall records. If you are unsure, clicking a Quick Demo Preset will autofill standard regional averages.
